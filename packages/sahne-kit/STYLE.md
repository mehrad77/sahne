# Sahne kit: style guide

The rules every piece of the first kit follows, so a new part made in a fresh session matches the rest. The gallery (`pnpm sahne dev`) is the visual reference; when this file and the gallery disagree, fix one of them.

What to draw comes from the catalogue: [`CATALOGUE.md`](CATALOGUE.md) lists every part drawn and planned (generated by `node packages/sahne-kit/catalogue/build.ts` from the kit, `catalogue/plan.json` and `src/curation.ts`; never edit it by hand). The plan is `catalogue/plan.json`: each planned part with what it is and how it looks. What a learner may pick is curated in `src/curation.ts`.

## Paper

- **Cut paper, not ink.** No outlines. Every shape is a flat fill with a lighter cut rim (`DEFAULT_EDGE`, 0.45 cm) and, when it lies on another shape of the same piece, a small soft shadow under it. Details that are printed on the paper rather than cut (blush, freckles, stripes, glints, painted shade) are `flat: true`: no rim, no shadow.
- **Light from the upper left.** Shade sits on the right and under things; highlights on the left and on top. Use the tone ramp from `sahne` (`tone(c, "shade" | "deep" | "light" | "edge")`), never hand-picked darker colours, so every colour shades the same way.
- **Grain** comes from the renderer; never draw texture into the art.
- **Sizes are centimetres** of the real thing: a loaf is 26 cm wide, a door 105 cm, an adult 160–186 cm.

## People

- **Proportions** (from `body()`): adults have a 50 cm head on a 160–186 cm body (about 1:3.4); elders are shorter with a 48 cm head; children are 106–122 cm with a 40 cm head; toddlers 88–98 cm with a 37 cm head.
- **Shapes:** slim, average, broad, round. Clothes are drawn from the body's measurements, never for one shape.
- **Faces carry the acting.** Big eyes (white with a deep rim, a large iris, a glint), lids that close and squint, two-point brows, a small soft nose, a mouth built from open / wide / round / smile. Keep the face clear of detail that would fight the expression.
- **Arms** hang in front of the torso, pinned at a shoulder that sits inside the torso's outline. Limbs are strips (`StripSpec`), so they bend; hands and feet are pieces.
- **Hands** are kit pieces with alternatives (`Item.alt`): relaxed, open, point, fist, hold, pinch, thumb and `count1`…`count5`. Counting starts with the index finger, as in Turkey and Iran; five is the open hand. Fingers are rounded capsules, the thumb a tapered piece, at most a couple of flat crease lines.
- **Gestures** are data (`gestures.ts`), written once for the acting side and mirrored. Each starts with a small anticipation, settles with a spring, and ends at rest. Hands that must arrive somewhere (a target, the chin, the chest) use `reach`, not fixed angles, so they fit every body. Gestures carry the culture: the *cık* is the "no", *beckon* is palm down, a hand on the heart says thanks.
- **Every part has a back view.** The paper flip shows it.
- **No gender gate.** Any part works on any body. Modest options are always present: headscarves, long sleeves, long skirts.
- **Crowds** (`src/set/people.ts`) are faceless paper people seen side on, muted towards the haze: real proportions (a 15 cm head on an adult, larger on a child), every skin tone, headscarves and caps as often as on a real Istanbul street, children beside a grown-up, elders with a stoop or a cane. They read frozen mid-stride, and never carry words.

## Colour

- People and clothes use only the palettes in `src/palette.ts`: 19 skins, 19 hair colours, 8 eye colours, 38 fabrics, 5 metals and 4 lens tints (`CATALOGUE.md` lists them).
- Sets use the `SET` colours and a small range of warm Istanbul façades.
- Each character in a scene should have a different dominant fabric family, so a 48 px silhouette tells them apart.

## Places

- A place is built from set pieces, each a flat at a real depth: street outside, wall with openings, furniture against the wall, the counter, the actors, props.
- The wall must fill the top of the wide framing; the floor must reach the bottom.
- Pieces that stand against a wall take a `drop` shadow; free-standing ones rely on the floor.
- **Furniture people use is at puppet scale.** The big heads put an adult's shoulders at about 1.05 m and the waist at about 0.85 m, so a counter or a table is about 86 cm high, not 98. Then a shopkeeper's hands and apron show above it and a point at the bread reads.
- A thing that may go missing (*yok*) gets an empty spot around it (the fridge's `clear`), so its dashed outline reads.

- **Where set pieces live.** In `src/set/`, one file per family (`base`, `airport`, `metro`, `props`, …), each a chunk that stays within 40 KB gzip; the shared helpers (`S`, `P`, `L`, `num`, `str`, `flip`, `holed`, `goes`, `copies`, `ridge`) are in `src/set/draw.ts`, never copied into a family. A new family is listed in `src/set/index.ts` and `all.ts`, then `node packages/sahne-kit/set/build.ts`.
- **Buildings** (`facade`, `frontage` in `src/set/street.ts`) are Istanbul's: plaster in warm tones over a stone base, cumba bays, iron balconies, shutters, AC units, laundry and dishes, a water tank on the roof; floors count over a ground floor (zemin kat). A shop's sign is words on the fascia; its shutter is a param, so one shopfront serves open and closed.
- **Landmarks** are stylised silhouettes drawn from description, never traced: the shape a person would recognise (Galata's cone, the bridge's towers and cable, the mosque's two slim minarets), little detail, partly self-lit (`lit` under 1) rather than a glow over a whole wall, which blurs into a box.
- **Resolution.** A set piece is drawn at `ppcm` ≤ 3 (a wall at 0.9, the street at 0.42), a prop at ≤ 16; no flat may need more than 2040 px across (`test/kit.test.ts`).
- **Each generator declares itself** in `meta` (`PieceDef.meta`): a line on what it draws, every param it reads with its kind (`enum` values, a number's range, `bool`, `text`, `color`, `list`, `object`, or two of them), its default when the code has a fixed one, the place states it answers to and its usual card ids. `SET.md` and the gallery's piece inspector are built from it. The rules are functions in `src/check.ts` (`sahne-kit/check`: `checkPiece`, `checkKit`), which the kit's tests and backstage's tools both run: each generator is drawn with every variant, enum value and flag through a params object that records what is read, and a param read but not declared, a variant setting an undeclared or illegal value, a flat that outgrows an atlas layer, a restless motion or a word outside its art fails. The doc comment above the code is for whoever edits the drawing. A param is never named like a place's own field of a piece (`x y z id seed in switch terms`) or of a prop (`at terms shown keep`): the stage would read it as that too (a gull's flying height, once `y`, is `alt`).

## Props

- A prop's origin is its bottom centre. In someone's hand it is drawn with `held: true`, its origin at the grip, the arm and hand over it: draw it as people carry it. Loaves stand on end in the crook of the arm, a little fanned and still countable, never as a bar held across the waist.
- Things that glow at night (windows across the street, bulbs) go in the piece's `emissive` group. Lit windows come on by turns (`litByTurns` in `src/set/draw.ts`, `glowAt`), never all at once; a far city's lights last. The sky's flats are `sky: true` and `switch: false`.
- Signs and labels with words are HTML overlays (DESIGN.md §6.8), never drawn into the art. A text's `lang` is its language tag (`fr` on a boulangerie's sign); left out, it is Turkish. A prop puts them in its flat's `text` (a box in the flat's centimetres), and the page's `propText` lays them out; `sign_board` and `phone` are the examples.

## Sound

Every sound is synthesized in code (`sounds.ts`, `music.ts`) from the patch format in `sahne` (`audio/dsp.ts`). A few have a recording that plays instead where it loads: `gull`, `ferry_horn`, `traffic` and `crowd` (`sounds/SOURCES.md`; each needs the owner's approval before download, DESIGN.md §8.3). Their patches stay, as the fallback. Nobody here can listen, so `test/sound.test.ts` checks every rule below by numbers; render a spectrogram before calling a new sound done.

- **Levels** (the patch's `peak`): effects 0.6–0.8; sounds that play on their own and often are quieter: `step`, `paper_flip`, `ui_tap` at 0.3–0.45, and a step on carpet at 0.025. Ambience 0.5, motifs 0.7. The engine's buses set the final volume; never level a sound by ear against another.
- **Rates:** effects and music at 32000 Hz, ambience at 22050.
- **Lengths:** a one-shot is as short as the thing it imitates (a step 0.1 s, a door 0.4–0.6 s, a bell or a horn up to 2.5 s). It starts at zero and dies to silence by its own envelopes: do not cut it with a `dur` shorter than its voices. The clock is the one exception: exactly 2 s, so it repeats in time.
- **Takes:** a sound the engine plays by itself and often has takes (`step` 4, `paper_flip` 3). A take varies filter frequencies, timing and level by about ±15 %, never so much that it reads as a different sound.
- **Randomness** comes only from the `rng` the patch is given, so a take is the same in every browser.
- **How a patch is written.** Build a sound from what makes it, with the helpers in `sounds.ts`:
  - an impact is a short noise burst for the contact (`hit`) plus struck partials for what rings (`ring`);
  - wood rings low, inharmonic and short; glass and brass ring high and long (`glass`, `bell`, `gong`);
  - partials are ratios over one frequency, so a take can move the pitch of the whole thing;
  - calls and beeps are `tone`s with a glide; voices, engines and horns are saws through filters.
  - voices (calls, singing, chatter, whispers) are textures, never words: saws through formant band-passes (`vox`, `choir`, `chat`, `laugh`), or noise through them for a whisper; a bare room's flutter echo is `echo`;
  - a `ring` renders only until its longest partial has died;
  - the periodic parts of a loop (an engine's beat, a hum) make whole cycles in the loop's length, so the seam holds.
- **Ambience** is a seamless loop of 6–10 s: a held noise bed moved by slow `noise` LFOs, and events (drops, gusts, laps, cars) at times drawn from the rng and placed with `scatter`, which repeats an event that crosses the loop's end so the seam stays continuous. No single event may stand out enough to be heard coming round again; keep the gaps between events uneven.
- **Budgets:** one take renders in Node in ≤ 25 ms (effects) and ≤ 80 ms (ambience loops, motifs). Keep `sounds.ts` and `music.ts` together under about 25 KB.

### Music

- **One key centre, D**, so every motif sits with every other. Bright motifs lean on Rast on D (D E F#~ G A B C), intimate ones on Uşşak on D (D E F G A Bb C).
- **Quarter tones with restraint:** the only one is Rast's *segah*, F#~, in a falling line (Kerim's theme and the ending, which share that descent).
- **No chords with thirds under a melody.** The pad plays a drone, D and A, as Turkish music does, so a modal note never clashes with the harmony.
- **Every motif ends resolved:** its melody (the first track) lands on D and rings into a short tail. Scene motifs are 2.5–4 s; the end stinger is about 2 s.
- **Gentle, never busy:** one melody, at most one accompaniment and a drone. Kerim's theme quotes the coo's short–LONG–short rhythm.
- **Instruments:** `saz` (a bağlama: two steel strings of a course a few cents apart, a plectrum tick), `ney` (weak harmonics, breath at the pitch, a late 5 Hz vibrato of 15 cents), `pad` (detuned triangles, slow in and out), `bell` (small and bright), `def` (`d` düm, `t` tek, `k` ka).
